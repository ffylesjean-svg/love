# Qualitative Research Integrity Rules

Read this module before analysing, revising, or verifying research prose. These rules override stylistic preferences and author-voice matching.

## 1. Keep the evidence ladder intact

Identify the evidential status of every claim before editing:

| Evidence level | Editing constraint |
| --- | --- |
| Participant speech | Protect the quotation and its attribution. |
| Retrospective account | Preserve that the account is remembered and narrated from the present. |
| Dataset pattern | Preserve the stated scope, frequency, and differences in the supplied material. |
| Researcher interpretation | Preserve the interpretive frame and calibrated claim strength. |
| Scholarship | Preserve source attribution, citation scope, and the supplied account of the source. |
| Uncertainty or limitation | Preserve the limit rather than removing it for fluency. |

Moving a statement to a stronger evidential level requires evidence and author authorisation. Style alone never justifies that movement.

### Retrospective attribution

Retrospective interview data are remembered and narrated from the present. Preserve verbs, attribution, and qualifiers that locate a claim in recollection. Never convert a participant's retrospective account into an unqualified historical fact.

## 2. Protect participant material

Unless the user explicitly authorises a clearly bounded change, participant quotations are locked text.

- Preserve wording, meaningful punctuation, hesitations, repetitions, pseudonyms, and attribution.
- Do not correct grammar merely for neatness.
- Do not combine quotations from different participants or interview moments.
- Do not invent bridging words or ellipses.
- Do not silently remove contradictions, ambivalence, or negative cases.
- Flag unclear, potentially mistranscribed, or difficult material for the researcher.

Quotation-adjacent prose may be revised within the authorised scope, but it must not tell a participant what they meant. Keep description and inference distinct.

### Translation

Treat supplied translations as protected unless the user explicitly requests translation review. Do not silently retranslate them. Preserve stated translation conventions, bilingual terms, translator notes, and distinctions between original speech and researcher rendering.

## 3. Preserve qualitative hedging

Hedging can mark responsible interpretation rather than weakness. Preserve the strength and evidential function of epistemic qualifiers, retrospective markers, bounded claims, and statements of uncertainty.

Remove or consolidate a hedge only when it is genuinely redundant and doing so does not strengthen the claim. Never make a qualitative interpretation sound like an established causal or population-level finding without supplied justification.

## 4. Protect citations and scholarship

Never:

- invent a source, page number, quotation, reference, or citation key;
- replace one scholar with another;
- strengthen a cited author's position beyond what the supplied manuscript says;
- delete a citation merely because surrounding prose changes;
- attach a citation to a new claim that has not been checked against the source;
- convert scholarly disagreement into false consensus.

Preserve citation placement unless a move is necessary for readability and its scope remains unambiguous. If support seems missing or weak, flag it for source checking rather than fabricating a repair.

## 5. Protect theoretical specificity

Named concepts are not ornamental synonyms.

- Preserve terminology associated with the manuscript's theoretical traditions.
- Do not replace a protected concept with a conversational near-synonym.
- Do not collapse distinctions among related concepts merely to vary vocabulary.
- Do not introduce theory because a descriptive passage appears to need depth.
- When theory is already present, clarify only analytical relations supported by the supplied text.

## 6. Preserve difference, disorder, and limits

- Preserve differences among participants, sites, periods, and contexts.
- Preserve exceptions, contradictions, negative cases, uncertainty, and temporal qualifiers.
- Do not make the dataset appear more coherent or representative than it is.
- Do not turn interpretive association into causation.
- Do not expand a claim from the sample to a population.
- Do not erase researcher positionality or methodological limitations for concision.

## 7. Protect factual tokens

During revision and verification, compare:

- participant pseudonyms and labels;
- quotation text;
- numbers, dates, ages, durations, and frequencies;
- sample size and participant characteristics;
- names of sites, policies, programmes, and organisations;
- citation keys, author-year citations, page numbers, and table or figure labels;
- named methods, analytical stages, and theoretical concepts.

Treat an unexplained mismatch as a failed integrity check unless the user authorised it.

## 8. Respond to ambiguity

When prose can carry more than one evidential meaning:

1. retain the narrower supported reading;
2. avoid converting an ambiguous substantive point into certainty;
3. flag the issue for the author;
4. label any conditional alternative with the assumption it requires.

Editing restraint is part of methodological integrity.
