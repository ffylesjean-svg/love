# Manuscript Section Editing Rules

Use the lowest editing intensity consistent with the user's defined scope and each section's function. The user may explicitly lower the intensity. A request for more stylistic intervention cannot relax research-integrity protections.

## Built-in intensity map

| Section | Default | Main focus | Strongest protections |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Introduction | MEDIUM | Generic openings, broad significance, mechanical roadmaps, repetitive framing | Research problem, scope, contribution, questions |
| Literature Review | MEDIUM | Citation dumping, author-by-author sequence, generic synthesis, unsupported generalisation | Differences among scholars, citation scope, contested concepts |
| Theoretical Framework or Resources | LIGHT–MEDIUM | Repetition, vague conceptual claims, theory name-dropping | Named concepts, internal distinctions, source attribution |
| Methodology | LIGHT | Clarity, procedural sequence, needless repetition | Design, recruitment, sampling, ethics, transcription, translation, analysis, positionality |
| Findings | VERY LIGHT–LIGHT | Obstructive repetition or generic recaps only | Quotations, attribution, pseudonyms, differences, negative cases, time, uncertainty, retrospective framing |
| Discussion | MEDIUM | Repetitive theory links, generic conclusions, recap, overclaiming | Evidence–interpretation–literature boundaries and limitations |
| Conclusion | MEDIUM | Inflated significance, generic implications, excessive repetition | Bounded contribution, limitations, caution |

## Introduction

- Replace generic importance claims only with a specific problem, context, or analytical gap already established in the manuscript.
- Keep necessary definitions and contextual framing.
- Vary roadmap language only when repetition obstructs reading.
- Do not enlarge the study's contribution.

## Literature Review

- Organise synthesis around a substantive relationship already represented in the source text.
- Preserve what each scholar is said to argue.
- Do not compress distinct traditions into consensus.
- Never invent a source to fill a perceived gap.
- Keep conventional citation clusters when their argumentative function is clear.

## Theoretical Framework or Resources

- Protect established theoretical terms and distinctions.
- Do not replace a concept with a generic synonym merely for stylistic variation.
- Clarify what a concept enables the analysis to identify only when that relation is already present.
- Do not add theoretical machinery to descriptive prose.

## Methodology

Methodology legitimately uses standard, procedural, and sometimes repetitive language. Do not revise it merely because it sounds conventional.

Protect design, rationale, recruitment, sampling, consent, confidentiality, ethics, transcription, translation, coding, analysis, positionality, sample information, dates, stages, software, and audit-trail details. Revise only for ambiguity, obstructive repetition, inaccurate sequence, or unnecessary metadiscourse. Never substitute a different method.

## Findings

Apply the strongest restraint.

- Preserve participant quotations verbatim unless the user authorises a bounded quotation change.
- Preserve attribution, pseudonyms, differences, contradictions, negative cases, temporal qualifiers, uncertainty, and retrospective framing.
- Do not add theory unless the existing findings section already uses it and a revision only clarifies an existing link.
- Do not make the data appear more coherent, representative, or causal than the supplied manuscript does.
- Prefer **KEEP** when repeated attribution avoids ambiguity about whose account is presented.

Do not reorder evidence or change emphasis without explicit authorisation.

## Discussion

- Reduce repeated analytical framing only when it obscures the reasoning.
- Clarify relationships among evidence, researcher interpretation, theoretical resources, and prior scholarship without creating new relationships.
- Remove generic recap that performs no analytical work.
- Preserve alternative readings, limitations, and claims bounded to the data.

## Conclusion

- Replace inflated implications with the study's actual bounded contribution when already supplied.
- Avoid unnecessary repetition of the manuscript structure.
- Preserve limitations, unresolved questions, and cautious implications.
- Do not promise policy effects or transferability not supported by the study.

## Mixed or unknown sections

When a passage spans sections, apply the more protective rule to each part according to its function. If the section cannot be inferred, default to LIGHT until the user or manuscript supplies enough context.

During the second pass, this intensity map affects judgement only; it never expands editing beyond mapped Turnitin-flagged regions.
