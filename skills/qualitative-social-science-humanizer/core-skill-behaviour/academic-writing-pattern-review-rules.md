# Academic Writing Pattern Review Rules

Use this module to diagnose and revise writing quality. It is not a word blacklist, a detector emulator, or a source of substitute prose.

## Governing rule

The normal unit of analysis is a paragraph or surrounding passage. Wording becomes a problem through its function, vagueness, repetition, or mismatch with evidence—not because an individual word appears on a list.

Before flagging a pattern, ask:

1. Does the wording communicate a specific point supported by the supplied evidence?
2. Is its form appropriate for the manuscript section and discipline?
3. Does the same rhetorical move recur mechanically nearby?
4. Would a change improve academic quality or only make the wording look different?
5. Would the change weaken a necessary hedge, term, attribution, quotation, or citation relationship?
6. Is the change consistent with the user's stored writing habits?

If a change would only make the wording look different, use **KEEP**.

## Preferred directions

When the supplied material supports revision, prefer:

- generic statements made analytically specific;
- abstract significance replaced by the actual supported implication;
- mechanical transitions replaced by the passage's real logical relationship;
- theory connected to an identifiable analytical function already present in the manuscript;
- generic summaries replaced by bounded interpretation of supplied evidence;
- paragraph structure determined by the argument rather than a repeated template.

These are directions, not writing templates. Never invent the missing specificity.

## Patterns to review

### Generic significance

Review evaluative claims whose importance, complexity, novelty, or relevance is asserted without identifying what the supplied evidence establishes. Keep them when nearby text supplies the required specificity or the term has a defined analytical function.

### Formulaic paragraph sequences

Review adjacent paragraphs that repeat the same order of claim, evidence, interpretation, theory, and recap without argumentative need. Repair only when the repetition obscures comparison, qualification, temporal change, or analytical development. Never merge participant voices or remove a negative case merely to vary structure.

### Repetitive transitions

Review repeated transitions when they fail to name a genuine relationship. Prefer an implicit link when logic is already clear or an explicit relationship grounded in contrast, extension, qualification, cause, consequence, or temporal movement. Do not ban individual transition words.

### Vague evaluative vocabulary

Review broad evaluative terms when the passage does not state what is being evaluated or why. Retain a term with a technical, contrastive, or source-attributed role.

### Overly symmetrical prose

Review repeated parallel structures or uniformly balanced clauses when the pattern becomes more prominent than the analysis. Keep parallelism that clarifies comparison or classification. Never add errors, fragments, or arbitrary length changes to simulate spontaneity.

### Abstract filler and recap

Review metadiscourse or recap that adds no logical, epistemic, or signposting function. Preserve a concise recap when it helps the reader move from dense evidence to interpretation or from one analytical stage to another.

### Superficial theory linking

Review passages that name a theoretical resource without showing what analytical relation it reveals in the supplied material. Do not add a theoretical claim, concept, or source during stylistic revision.

### Repeated appended interpretation

Review sentences that repeatedly attach generic interpretive clauses after otherwise complete claims. Revise only when a more precise relationship is already supported. Keep the form when its subject and inferential relation are clear.

### Inflated certainty or scope

Review verbs, quantifiers, causal language, and population-level claims that exceed the evidence described in the manuscript. Calibrate to the supplied evidence; do not add automatic hedging to a claim that is already warranted.

### Citation-shaped summary without synthesis

In literature reviews, review author-by-author summaries when the organising relationship among studies is missing. Improve the relation already available in the manuscript while preserving what each source is said to argue. Never invent a source or resolve a disagreement for smoother prose.

### Generic openings, roadmaps, and conclusions

Review broad openings, mechanical roadmaps, inflated implications, and conclusions that repeat rather than synthesise. Keep standard signposting when document length, genre, or institutional guidance makes it useful.

## Priority guide

| Priority | Use when |
| --- | --- |
| **HIGH** | The passage obscures analysis, overstates evidence, misuses theory, or repeats a structure that materially impairs the section. |
| **MEDIUM** | A bounded revision would clearly improve specificity, logic, or rhythm without altering substance. |
| **LOW** | The pattern is noticeable but harmless, local, or mainly cosmetic; normally preserve it. |
| **KEEP** | The language is academically justified, evidence-calibrated, author-consistent, section-appropriate, or protected. |

Use **KEEP** as a reasoned editorial decision.
