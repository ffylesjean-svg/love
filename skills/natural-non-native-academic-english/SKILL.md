# Natural, Accurate, Not Over-Nativised Academic English
自然、准确、不过度母语化的学术英语

**Goal:** Produce clear and publishable academic English that sounds consistent with an actual multilingual scholar's own prose. It is **not** a simulated accent, a fabricated second-language identity, an intentional grammar-error generator, or a detector-evasion method.

## Sources and routing
- Start with [Qualitative Social Science Humanizer](../qualitative-social-science-humanizer/SKILL.md), particularly its integrity and user-writing-sample-analysis modules, **when the author provides a verifiably genuine earlier writing sample**. That upstream skill requires a genuine sample and does not allow a substitute.
- Supplement with [Prose Quality Review](../prose-quality-review/SKILL.md) and [Academic Research & Human Voice](../academic-research-human-voice/SKILL.md) for evidence, terminology and argument flow.
- If the author has not supplied a reliable personal writing sample, do **not** claim the strict upstream voice-learning mode has run. Instead, use this narrower **minimal-edit mode** on the text provided: fix clear errors and template-language problems but do not invent or imitate a hypothetical "Chinese ESL" style. Ask for genuine samples only when an author-profile match is necessary; do not block ordinary proofreading.
- For percentages, frequencies and survey reporting, additionally consult [Descriptive Statistics & Human Voice](../descriptive-statistics-human-voice/SKILL.md).
- Do not send private writing samples to a public repository or external destination. Keep any author voice profile in an authorized private project or the conversation, not this Skill library.

## Editing constraints
1. **Correct grammar is required**. Fix real errors in articles, agreement, tense, pronouns, prepositions, ambiguous reference and sentence completeness. Never introduce errors to evoke "non-native" writing.
2. **Prefer the author's words and choices**. If a sentence is already accurate, natural enough and appropriately academic, leave it unchanged; fluency for its own sake is not a benefit.
3. **British academic English as default**, unless publication instructions require another variety.
4. **No automatic "upgrade" to native-speaker idioms**. Avoid ornamental literary metaphors, promotional adjectives, stock transitions, idioms the author does not use, and long Latinate substitutes for plain verbs.
5. **No artificial vocabulary ceiling**. Do not impose IELTS word limits or forbid difficult words. Established humanities and social-science terms must remain precise: for example *affect*, *jouissance*, *interpellation*, *mediation*, *seriality*, *animatic configuration*, *neoformalism*, *intercultural dialogue*. Replace only a word that is demonstrably vague, inaccurate, unnecessary or inconsistent in this context.
6. **Keep complexity where the thought requires it**. A multilingual writer may use substantial theoretical syntax, nominalisations, hedges, subordination and repeated key terms. Do not turn an intricate argument into chatty short sentences merely to alter surface rhythm.
7. **Preserve author stance and evidential status**. Keep quotations, attribution, sample sizes, figures, citations, theoretical distinctions, methodological limitations, uncertainty, modal verbs and descriptive-vs-causal boundaries. Do not change what the author claims.
8. **No artificial roughness**. Do not insert awkward phrasing, typos, unusual punctuation, invented first-person stories, sentence-length randomness, stereotypes about L1 Chinese authors, or other features to make text appear "less AI".
9. **Protect section function**. A Results paragraph describes findings; Discussion interprets; methodology explains what was actually done; conceptual analysis keeps warranted abstraction.
10. **Follow journal or university AI-use policies** and do not claim an AI-detection score change.

## Workflow
**A. Intake:** Read target text and task; identify genre (RP, journal article, conference abstract, critical essay, reviewer response), audience and relevant British/venue conventions. Identify original author samples, if actually supplied, and whether upstream's strict author-sample gate is met.

**B. Meaning lock:** Mark terms, citations, material claims, numbers and uncertainty that may not change. When there is no access to underlying data or sources, retain the given claim but mark any factual verification as not performed.

**C. Diagnose locally:** Look for ungrounded praise and inflated claims, mechanical openings, unnecessarily formal verbs, repetitive signposting, over-symmetric clauses, wordiness, genuine grammar issues, unclear antecedents and content-free recaps. Treat these as editorial observations, not proof of AI authorship.

**D. Apply a light edit before a heavy rewrite:** Keep the author's original subject, verbs and sentence structure whenever they are intelligible and accurate. Replace only what obstructs academic clarity or carries no content. Prefer small changes over generic "polishing".

**E. Author profile if available:** Use at least one clearly author-written genuine academic sample, prioritise samples near the target genre, extract tendencies rather than reusable sentences, distinguish author choices from mistakes and venue requirements. Never use AI-generated text or quotes as evidence of the author's personal voice.

**F. Verify:** Compare original and edited texts for differences in theory, attribution, scope, numbers, dates, quotations, citations, strength of claims and rhetorical stance. Explain substantive concerns separately rather than silently rewriting them.

## Output modes
- **默认润色**：直接提供完整修改稿 + 最多一小段解释。原文本来合适的地方不强行修改。
- **只检查**：仅标出实质性、语法或逻辑问题及位置，不擅自重写。
- **逐段修改**：按用户指定段落修改，不迁移其他段落。
- **双版本比较**：仅在用户明确要求时提供“最小润色”与“较充分润色”两版；默认最小润色优先。
- **文风档案**：仅使用真正的作者本人旧稿，保留来源和可信度限制；未经授权不上传或发布作者文字。

## Example (invented wording for illustration, NOT research findings)
Over-polished:
> This paper seeks to illuminate the intricate and multifaceted interplay between media circulation and the dynamic construction of shared cultural belonging.

Natural academic alternative:
> This paper examines how media circulation shapes a sense of shared cultural belonging, and where that sense of belonging remains limited.

The alternative is appropriate only when it preserves the writer's actual argument. Keep a technical term if its theoretical specificity demands it; do not substitute "belonging" for another concept such as *sharedness* without a reason.
